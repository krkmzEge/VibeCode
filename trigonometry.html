The unit circle as a storyteller

The best way to remember trig is to see the unit circle as a simple animation that produces two outputs at once.

Cosine = x-position

The horizontal coordinate of the endpoint tells you cos θ immediately. Watch how it slides left and right.

Sine = y-position

The vertical coordinate is sin θ. The projection on the right mirrors the sine graph scrolling in time.

Phase = angle

Every 360° loop brings the point back to the start, encoding periodicity.

Linking animation to identities

01

Trace projections

Follow the moving dot along the sine projection. The horizontal component is cos θ, vertical is sin θ.

02

Form the Pythagorean identity

Since the radius length is 1, \( \sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1 \) simply states that the point lies on the circle.

03

Understand phase shifts

Dragging the angle forward by 90° swaps sine and cosine positions, explaining addition formulas visually.

Animation tip: pause the animation when the dot crosses quadrants, then write the signs of sin θ and cos θ. You just derived the ASTC mnemonic straight from motion.
