What the histogram is really telling you

The galton-board inspired stats card shows a histogram updating with each dropped sample. Use this translation table to read it like a pro:

Bar height → frequency

Taller means the outcome has occurred more often. Over time bars arrange themselves into the underlying probability curve.

Width → bucket size

Narrow bins capture detail but look noisy; wide bins smooth things out but may hide features.

Color → sample age

In the interactive chart, lighter shades indicate recent samples so trends are easy to track.

Core concepts that appear in the demo

Law of large numbers

The sample average drifts toward the true mean as you drop more balls. The animation labels the running mean so you can watch it converge.

Central limit theorem

Even if the pegs send the balls randomly, the resulting distribution looks bell-shaped. This is why histograms often tend toward normal curves.

Variance intuition

Variance is the average squared distance from the mean. In the visualization, it controls how wide the histogram appears.

Workflow hint: drop a handful of samples → pause and note which bar is tallest → predict the next relative ordering → release a stream and check the histogram’s new shape.
