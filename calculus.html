What you will learn

A derivative answers “How quickly is this thing changing right now?” Rather than memorizing rules, we start with slopes of secant lines and shrink them into tangents.

Limit definition

The derivative of \(f(x)\) at \(x=a\) is the limit of the slope between \(a\) and \(a+h\) as \(h\) approaches 0.

Motion interpretation

Think of the moving pink point. Its vertical speed is exactly the derivative you compute.

Derivative rules

Power, sum, and chain rules appear as shortcuts after you understand the limiting picture.

Step-by-step walkthrough

01

Start with a sketch and prediction

Look at the graph of \(f(x)=\sin x \cdot x\). Before calculating, guess if the slope is positive or negative at the point we care about.

02

Set up the difference quotient

\(\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}\) is the slope between two nearby points. Plug the function values in but keep it symbolic as long as possible.

03

Take the limit and interpret

After simplifying, send \(h \to 0\). The result is the instantaneous slope. Compare it to the tangent line illustrated in the animation.
